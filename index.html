SPIRALIS
Editorial 2026

Spiralis is a magazine that explores how materials respond to external forces over time, focusing on the process of decay. In a society that constantly strives for perfection, the project reframes decay as something valuable: a process of transformation that gives matter new meanings and qualities. The magazine itself gradually undergoes this process. Its pages yellow, the ink fades, and the images slowly lose their intensity. In this way, the reader experiences decay firsthand while discovering new layers of knowledge. The spiral, which defines the magazine’s visual language, symbolises the cyclical and ongoing transformation that every object inevitably undergoes.

COEVO
Editorial 2025

Coevo explores how technology is often portrayed as a threat, particularly to younger generations who face the future with a growing sense of anxiety and uncertainty. The project challenges this narrative, suggesting that technology is not inherently the problem, but that its impact depends on how we understand it, use it, and choose to engage with it. The magazine creates a space for reflection where nature, humans, and technology are not treated as separate entities, but as interconnected parts of the same ecosystem—coexisting, influencing one another, and evolving together. Rather than offering a definitive answer about the future, Coevo brings together different perspectives and invites readers to question their own relationship with technology and form their own view of what lies ahead.

A tiny playground
Animation 2026

A playful exploration of stationery as a gateway to imagination. Everyday objects become characters, letters, and tools for creating imaginary worlds. Through photography, a typeface made from real stationery objects, risograph printing, and stop motion, the project brings these everyday objects to life and celebrates the freedom and joy of making. The visual language embraces a handmade, tactile, and intentionally imperfect aesthetic, highlighting the textures and physical qualities of the materials.

Kuchidoke
Typography 2026

For this project, I researched traditional Japanese sweets, travelling to Kyoto to explore the history of rakugan and the long-established shops that still make them today. Inspired by old handwritten menus and the visual language of these places, I designed a typeface based on rakugan and carved it into wood to create my own mould for making the sweets. I documented the process through photography and created a type specimen explaining the typeface, the sweets, and the mould. The specimen was entirely printed by hand using a risograph, which I learned to operate and used to recreate the soft, powdery quality of rakugan.

Kirinoki
Brand identity 2026

An identity designed to highlight an authentic and slower way of experiencing Tokyo, away from crowds and tourist destinations. Kirinoki invites visitors to discover local life through cafés, restaurants, seasonal activities, and small everyday rituals, creating a sense of familiarity and belonging. The logo draws from the family crest, while the soft blue of the identity evokes the calm atmosphere of the experience. The project includes logo design, illustration, photography, video editing, and motion design, telling the story of the Airbnb and its neighbourhood through the changing seasons.

Neptuna
Brand identity 2023

Neptuna is a speculative design project that imagines food consumption in 2050, in a scenario shaped by overfishing, marine pollution, and a growing demand for food. Through a fictional brand of tuna-based snacks and an interactive vending machine, the project transforms the act of purchasing into a critical experience: ironic and slightly unsettling. Packaging, receipts, and informative content reveal the hidden consequences of everyday consumption, inviting users to reflect on eating habits, sustainability, and the future of the ocean.

Vesuvio Spontaneo
Brand identity 2026

A speculative design project addressing illegal construction in the Vesuvio area through a political provocation: a fictional party that transforms illegal buildings into “cultural heritage” worthy of protection. Through AI-generated content, posters, slogans, and Instagram posts, the campaign constructs a credible yet paradoxical politician, using irony and pop language to reveal how political communication can manipulate real issues. The documentary component, based on articles, data, and research, brings the project back to its concrete urgency: volcanic risk, institutional responsibility, and the normalization of illegality.
